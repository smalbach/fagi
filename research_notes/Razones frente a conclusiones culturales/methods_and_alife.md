Research date: 2026-09-28.

## Q1. Has counterfactual / interventional replay (removing exactly one transmitted message) been used to measure the causal effect of a single cultural transmission event?

### Takeaway
The general ingredients are well established: "replaying the tape" from saved history points (Blount/Lenski; Avida-style digital evolution; a 2026 evolutionary-computation guide), counterfactual credit assignment in MARL (COMA; Jaques et al. social influence), and, very recently (2026), intervention-and-re-execute attribution of single steps in LLM-agent trajectories (Causal Agent Replay, CausalFlow). I found **no** source that uses exact deterministic replay of a whole multi-generation agent society with *one inter-agent cultural transmission (message) deleted/altered* to measure that message's causal effect on later beliefs. The novelty claim is plausible but should be phrased as "combination + unit of intervention (a single social-learning event) + exactness from determinism", not as the invention of counterfactual replay.

### Cited Findings
- **Replay experiments in experimental evolution (origin of the method).** Blount, Borland & Lenski (2008), "Historical contingency and the evolution of a key innovation in an experimental population of *Escherichia coli*", PNAS 105(23):7899–7906, doi:10.1073/pnas.0803151105. Cit+ evolved in one of 12 populations by ~31,500 generations; replays from frozen clones showed Cit+ could only re-evolve from later ancestors, i.e., it depended on prior "potentiating" mutations. — [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0803151105); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18524956/)
- Review: "Contingency and determinism in evolution: Replaying life's tape", Science (doi:10.1126/science.aam5979) — [Science](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aam5979). Authors Blount, Lenski & Losos (2018) **[UNVERIFIED authorship/year; only URL/title seen]**. Gould's "replaying the tape" thought experiment originates in *Wonderful Life* (1989) **[UNVERIFIED]**.
- **Replay in digital evolution / evolutionary computation (closest methodological cousin).** Ferguson & Lalejini (arXiv 2608.09833, Aug 2026), "Identifying potentiating events in evolutionary search using replay experiments": introduces "analytical replay experiments" to evolutionary computing; restarting a population from different historical time points samples what could have evolved; shows potential to evolve a solution can rise sharply while best fitness stays flat; gives a step-by-step guide. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09833); [html](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.09833v1) (snippet only; full text blocked)
- Ferguson & Ofria (2023), "Potentiating Mutations Facilitate the Evolution of Associative Learning in Digital Organisms" (ALIFE 2023 per author page **[venue UNVERIFIED]**) — replay experiments in Avida-like digital organisms evolving associative learning; code at GitHub. — [PDF](http://austinjferguson.com/docs/ferguson_ofria_2023_potentiating_mutations_facilitate_learning.pdf); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/374078716_Potentiating_Mutations_Facilitate_the_Evolution_of_Associative_Learning_in_Digital_Organisms); [GitHub](https://github.com/FergusonAJ/replaying_evolution_of_learning)
  - Note: these replays restart *stochastically* from a time point to estimate a probability distribution; they do not delete a single event and re-run deterministically. Fagi's "knock-out one message, exact replay" is a different (event-level, deterministic) intervention.
- **Counterfactual credit assignment in MARL.** Foerster, Farquhar, Afouras, Nardelli & Whiteson (2018), "Counterfactual Multi-Agent Policy Gradients" (COMA), AAAI-32: centralised critic with a counterfactual baseline that marginalises out a single agent's action while holding others fixed; StarCraft micromanagement. — [AAAI OJS](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/11794); [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.5555/3504035.3504398)
- Jaques, Lazaridou, Hughes, Gulcehre, Ortega, Strouse, Leibo & de Freitas (2019), "Social Influence as Intrinsic Motivation for Multi-Agent Deep Reinforcement Learning", ICML 2019, PMLR 97. At each timestep an agent simulates alternative actions and computes their effect on other agents' behaviour (counterfactual causal influence); equivalent to rewarding mutual information between actions. Influence is measured one step ahead via a learned model of other agents, not by replaying a full history. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/jaques19a.html); [arXiv 1810.08647](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.08647)
- **Intervention-and-re-execute attribution in LLM-agent traces (2026, closest in mechanics).** Shah (arXiv 2606.08275, June 2026), "Causal Agent Replay: Counterfactual Attribution for LLM-Agent Failures": models an agent run as an SCM, applies a do-operation to one step, re-executes forward under the same stochastic policy, measures the shift in the outcome distribution; includes an intervention algebra, single-step contrastive estimator, and Monte-Carlo Shapley credit splitting. Scope is single LLM-agent failure debugging, not cultural transmission. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08275)
  - Related 2026 preprints seen only as search titles: "CausalFlow: Causal Attribution and Counterfactual Repair for LLM Agent Failures" (arXiv 2605.25338); "Localizing Emergent Failures in Agentic AI: Recovering Minimal Repair Families via Counterfactual Replay" (arXiv 2608.29228); "Causal Attribution for Agentic Decisions: Estimators, Coupling, and a Traceability Specification" (arXiv 2609.06445); "MACIE: Multi-Agent Causal Intelligence Explainer for Collective Behavior Understanding" (arXiv 2511.15716). — [CausalFlow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25338); [Localizing](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.29228); [Causal Attribution](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.06445); [MACIE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.15716) **[contents UNVERIFIED beyond titles/snippets]**
  - The search-summary description of the general technique: "deleting, replacing, or neutralizing" an event, re-executing from that point and comparing factual vs counterfactual. — [Causal Agent Replay html](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08275v1)
- ABM and causal inference in epidemiology: "DAG-informed regression modelling, agent-based modelling and microsimulation modelling: a critical comparison of methods for causal inference", Int. J. Epidemiology 48(1):243 (2019) — frames ABMs as tools for counterfactual/causal reasoning at population level. — [IJE](https://academic.oup.com/ije/article/48/1/243/5231935)

### Inferences
- Fagi's method sits at the intersection of (a) Lenski/Blount replay (history-dependence, potentiation) and (b) SCM-style do-interventions on single steps (CAR 2026). The distinctive bits are: the intervened unit is a *social transmission event* (message between agents, with trust), the replay is *exact* (deterministic simulator, common random numbers in effect), and the outcome is a *belief* (and its descendants) across generations. Recommend citing Blount 2008, Ferguson & Lalejini 2026, Jaques 2019, COMA 2018 and CAR 2026 as the direct lineage, and framing Fagi as "event-level knock-out replay for cultural transmission".
- Determinism is also a limitation reviewers will raise: a single exact counterfactual gives the effect *in that trajectory* (an individual-level/actual-cause effect), not an average effect. A strong paper would pair exact knock-outs with seed ensembles (as Ferguson & Lalejini and CAR do distributionally) and report both. "Butterfly effect" chaos after a removal will make naive diffs large; CAR's "point-of-commitment locus rule" addresses a related run-forward confound.
- The "actual causation" literature (Halpern & Pearl; Halpern 2016 *Actual Causality*) **[UNVERIFIED, not searched]** is the natural formal frame for single-event knock-outs and should be checked.

## Q2. Phylogenies / genealogies of cultural traits in simulation, and of false beliefs specifically

### Takeaway
Phylogeny tracking is a mature ALife practice for *genomes* (Dolson/Moreno/Ofria's Phylotrack and hstrat), and misinformation ABMs are abundant, but I found no ALife or ABM work that records a full per-belief provenance genealogy of *false* beliefs ("myths") at the level of individual transmission events. This appears to be a genuine gap; Fagi can borrow phylogeny-tracking tooling and metrics directly.

### Cited Findings
- Dolson, Rodriguez-Papa, Moreno et al., "Phylotrack: C++ and Python libraries for in silico phylogenetic tracking" (arXiv 2405.09389, 2024; published in JOSS **[venue UNVERIFIED]**). Phylotracklib (header-only C++, part of Empirical) + phylotrackpy (Python wrapper); API to attach phylogenetic tracking to digital evolution systems and compute phylogenetic topology metrics. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09389); [GitHub](https://github.com/emilydolson/phylotrackpy)
- hstrat (Moreno et al.): Python package for decentralized phylogenetic inference ("hereditary stratigraphy") in distributed digital evolution. — [search result listing; tutorial](https://emilydolson.github.io/alife-phylogeny-tutorial/)
- Tutorial "Phylogenies: how and why to track them in artificial life" (Dolson et al.): phylogenies expose ecology, differential selection, genetic potentiation, emergence of complex traits. — [Tutorial](https://emilydolson.github.io/alife-phylogeny-tutorial/)
- Misinformation ABMs (no event-level genealogy): Tambuscio et al. (fact-checking vs hoax spreading), Del Vicario et al. (confirmation bias) summarised in "Controlling the Misinformation Diffusion in Social Media by the Effect of Different Classes of Agents" (arXiv 2401.11524); "Cognitive cascades: How to model (and potentially counter) the spread of fake news", PLOS ONE 2022, which notes disease-style models lack internal belief models. — [arXiv 2401.11524](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.11524); [PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0261811); [Unito DSAA 2019 PDF](https://iris.unito.it/retrieve/564ed016-c4b5-4241-9fb9-0abe0904ddb2/Simulation%20of%20misinformation%20spreading%20processes_DSAA2019.pdf)
- Transmission-chain paradigm with LLMs (tracking distortion of content across "generations"): Acerbi & Stubbersfield (2023), "Large language models show human-like content biases in transmission chain experiments", PNAS 120(44), doi:10.1073/pnas.2313790120. ChatGPT-3 reproduces human biases (gender-stereotype-consistent, negative, social, threat-related, biologically counterintuitive content). — [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2313790120)
- Cultural phylogenetics (Mace, Gray, Tehrani etc.) exists for real-world data **[UNVERIFIED; not searched this session]**.

### Inferences
- A "myth genealogy" is essentially a transmission tree over belief instances; phylotrackpy-style data structures (taxa = belief variants, edges = transmission events with provenance tags own-bite/told/taught) could be reused, and standard phylogenetic metrics (e.g., diversity, depth, Colless) applied to myths. That would make the contribution legible to ALife reviewers.
- Combining genealogy with knock-out replay yields a natural "which ancestor transmission was pivotal for this myth" analysis — analogous to identifying potentiating mutations (Blount 2008; Ferguson & Lalejini 2026), but for false beliefs.

### Gaps
- No source found for simulations that specifically trace lineages of *false* beliefs with explicit provenance; absence of evidence, not proof.

## Q3. Closest artificial-life / game / AI systems

### Takeaway
Closest ancestors for the *food-toxicity learning with cues + evolution* part are Todd & Miller (1991) and Ackley & Littman (1991); for *simulated creatures with biochemistry, learning and generations* Creatures (Grand et al. 1996–97) and PolyWorld (Yaeger 1994); for *cultural transmission across generations in learning agents* DeepMind's Bhoopchand et al. (2023) and Cook et al. (2024); for *LLM societies transmitting information* Park et al. (2023), Vallinder & Hughes (2024), Acerbi & Stubbersfield (2023). None combines symbolic, provenance-carrying, self-explaining beliefs with deterministic counterfactual replay.

### Cited Findings
- **Todd & Miller (1991)**, "Exploring adaptive agency II: Simulating the evolution of associative learning", in *From Animals to Animats: Proc. 1st Int. Conf. on Simulation of Adaptive Behavior* (SAB 1990), MIT Press. - **Ackley & Littman (1991/1992)**, "Interactions between learning and evolution", in Langton, Taylor, Farmer & Rasmussen (eds.), *Artificial Life II*, Addison-Wesley, pp. 487–509. Evolutionary reinforcement learning (ERL): evolved innate evaluation network guides lifetime RL. — [UT Austin bib entry](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~shivaram/readings/b2hd-AckleyLittman1992.html); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Interactions-between-learning-and-evolution-Ackley-Littman/675be3c1f8a57015a91be5cd191a8d262a9061fb)

### Inferences
- Todd & Miller 1991 should be cited as the canonical "evolving learners that must tell food from poison via unreliable cues" model; Fagi extends it with (i) Rescorla–Wagner + symbolic rule induction, (ii) social transmission with trust, (iii) environmental inversion (a classic test of "lock-in" of cultural maladaptation), and (iv) provenance/explanations.
- Creatures is the closest *game* precedent (biochemistry-driven creatures, generations, genetics) but Norn learning is subsymbolic and not socially transmitted with provenance.
- LLM-society papers measure aggregate outcomes; none (found) does exact single-message knock-out replay, and LLM stochasticity makes exact replay hard — Fagi's determinism is a real methodological advantage worth emphasising.
