## Q1. Derex, Bonnefon, Boyd & Mesoudi (2019) and its follow-ups, replications and critiques

### Takeaway
Derex et al. (2019) is the canonical human experiment in which one condition passes on a written causal *theory* along with the *solutions* (wheel configurations and their scores). Adding the theory did not speed up improvement. Theories tended to be simple and one-dimensional, and when they were inaccurate they narrowed what the next learner explored. Between 2021 and 2026 the finding was challenged (Osiurak et al.), critiqued on method (Högberg et al., 2024), extended to show that transmitted theories "canalize" exploration whether they are right or wrong (Derex et al., 2025), and refined (Saral, Ibáñez de Aldecoa & Derex, 2026: causal reasoning speeds up early improvement but its advantage fades). No study in this line manipulates *environmental change*. The wheel physics stays fixed.

### Cited Findings
- **Citation:** Derex M., Bonnefon J.-F., Boyd R., Mesoudi A. (2019). "Causal understanding is not necessary for the improvement of culturally evolving technology." *Nature Human Behaviour* 3(5): 446–452. doi:10.1038/s41562-019-0567-9. Published April 2019. — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-019-0567-9); [ASU Pure](https://asu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/causal-understanding-is-not-necessary-for-the-improvement-of-cult/); [PLOS One summary citing it](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0310503)
- **Task.** Participants minimize the time a wheel takes to roll about 1 m down an inclined track. The wheel has four radial spokes, each with a weight that can be moved along it. Speed depends on (a) the moment of inertia (weights near the hub are faster) and (b) the position of the centre of mass (unbalanced, with the mass ahead of and above the axle, is better for initial acceleration). — [CNRS press release](https://www.cnrs.fr/en/press/it-not-necessary-understand-technology-order-improve-it); [Högberg et al. 2024 PLOS One](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0310503)
- **Design.** Chains of 5 participants, one per "generation". Each participant gets 5 attempts, then answers an understanding questionnaire about which physical factors affect speed. In the second version, each participant passes on their last two configurations with scores **plus a written text of their theory** of what makes the wheel fast. — [CNRS](https://www.cnrs.fr/en/press/it-not-necessary-understand-technology-order-improve-it); [Högberg et al. 2024](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0310503)
- **Findings.** The wheel improved across generations without any improvement in causal understanding. Passing on causal theories "had no noticeable effect on the pace of cultural evolution" because participants "did not spontaneously create multidimensional causal theories but instead produced simplistic models", and "the transmission of these inaccurate theories constrained learners' exploration." — [search summary of abstract](https://asu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/causal-understanding-is-not-necessary-for-the-improvement-of-cult/); [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-019-0567-9)
- **[UNVERIFIED design detail]** I recall about 14 chains per condition (configurations-only vs configurations+theory), and that learners who received a theory focused on one dimension (for example inertia only) understood the other dimension less well. Check this against the full text.
- **Osiurak F., et al. (2022).** "Technical reasoning bolsters cumulative technological culture through convergent transformations." *Science Advances* 8(9), eabl7446, 2 March 2022. Microsociety experiment on the same wheel system with "partial- and degraded-information transmission conditions". The wheel was optimized over generations *together with* growing understanding. Participants transferred that understanding to other problems and outperformed controls. The authors describe it as a partial replication of Derex et al. (2019), "motivated by methodological issues". — [Science Advances](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.abl7446); [PubMed 35235360](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35235360/)
- **Osiurak et al. (2021)**, "Technical reasoning is important for cumulative technological culture", *Nature Human Behaviour* (doi:10.1038/s41562-021-01159-9). A second paper in the same line, with conditions such as "Speed-Only" and "Configurations+Speed+Noise". — [Nature Human Behaviour](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-021-01159-9); [author blog post](https://communities.springernature.com/posts/technical-reasoning-is-important-to-avoid-reinventing-the-wheel). The **full author list and volume/pages are UNVERIFIED**.
- **Osiurak et al. (2022), Trends in Cognitive Sciences**, "Bringing cumulative technological culture beyond copying versus reasoning" (S1364-6613(22)00245-5). A review and position paper. — [Cell TICS](https://www.cell.com/trends/cognitive-sciences/abstract/S1364-6613(22)00245-5)
- **Micro-society model paper (2022).** "Impact of technical reasoning and theory of mind on cumulative technological culture: insights from a model of micro-societies", *Humanities and Social Sciences Communications*. Authors not verified; probably the Osiurak group. — [HSSC](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-022-01251-z)
- **Critique: Högberg A., Lombard M., Högberg A., Iliefski-Janols E., Lindblad G., Almér A., Thompson W.H., Rost M., Andreasson S., Wiig A., Gärdenfors P. (2024).** "Human socio-technical evolution through the lens of an abstracted-wheel experiment: A critical look at a micro-society laboratory study." *PLOS One*, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0310503. This is a third-generation run of the wheel paradigm. Earlier groups reached "fundamentally different outcomes": social learning only (Derex) versus technical reasoning (Osiurak). The authors conclude that the paradigm "reflects behavioral output only, instead of testing for cognition". — [PLOS One](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0310503)
- **Follow-up: Derex M., Bonnefon J.-F., Boyd R., McElreath R., Mesoudi A. (2025).** "Social learning preserves both useful and useless theories by canalizing learners' exploration." *Proceedings of the Royal Society B* 292(2039): 20242499, doi:10.1098/rspb.2024.2499.
  - Same wheel task. Participants were given theories "ranging from accurate to misleading".
  - "Even under conditions conducive to exploration, the transmission of cultural knowledge canalizes learners' focus, limiting their consideration of alternative solutions."
  - The effect appeared both in the theories participants produced and in the solutions they tried, "irrespective of the accuracy of the provided theories".
  - This is the closest direct human evidence that **transmitted reasons can lock in false beliefs**, although the environment does not change.
  - Sources: [Proc B](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rspb.2024.2499); [TSE WP 1618](https://www.tse-fr.eu/sites/default/files/TSE/documents/doc/wp/2025/wp_tse_1618.pdf); [search summary](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rspb/article/292/2039/20242499/104855/Social-learning-preserves-both-useful-and-useless)
- **Follow-up: Saral A.S.\*, Derex M.\* (equal contribution), Ibáñez de Aldecoa P. (2026).** "The roles of cultural transmission and causal reasoning in the cultural evolution of technology." *PNAS Nexus* 5(4): pgag090, published about 26 March 2026.
  - Two transmission-chain experiments, 900 participants, three conditions: (i) technical reasoning, (ii) causal reasoning, (iii) pure cultural transmission with the causal structure removed.
  - Cumulative improvement happened even without causal structure.
  - Causal reasoning sped up early improvement, but "its impact diminished over time".
  - Technical reasoning added nothing beyond causal reasoning.
  - Conclusion: causal reasoning is "beneficial yet dispensable".
  - Sources: [PNAS Nexus](https://academic.oup.com/pnasnexus/article/5/4/pgag090/8541945); [PubMed 41978574](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41978574/)
- **Field evidence: Harris J.A., Boyd R., Wood B.M. (2021).** "The role of causal knowledge in the evolution of traditional technology." *Current Biology* (S0960-9822(21)00161-5). Hadza bow makers hold *incomplete* causal theories: correct about how thickness affects arrow speed, but not about cross-section shape and energy storage. Over 80% said they got their causal theories culturally, about 10% from personal experience. This shows that reasons, including partly false ones, are themselves culturally transmitted. — [Current Biology](https://www.cell.com/current-biology/fulltext/S0960-9822(21)00161-5); [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2021-02-aim-evolution-traditional-technologies.html)
- **Related (2026):** Yaman A., Tian S., Lindström B., "Semantic knowledge guides innovation and drives cultural evolution", *PNAS* (doi:10.1073/pnas.2530750123; arXiv 2510.12837). Agent-based model plus experiment (N = 1,243). Participants without semantic knowledge performed at chance even when social information was available. — [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2530750123); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.12837)

### Inferences
- The wheel literature is the direct empirical test of "reasons vs conclusions". The accumulated answer is:
  - conclusions (configurations and scores) are enough for improvement;
  - adding reasons gives at most an early speed-up (Saral et al., 2026);
  - reasons canalize exploration and preserve useless or false theories as readily as useful ones (Derex et al., 2019, 2025).
  This points to the **"more trapped" side**, but only in a *static* environment.
- The information budget is not controlled. The theory arm receives configurations **plus** a theory. None of these studies compares theory-only with configuration-only at equal information content.

### Gaps
- Exact numbers of chains and participants, and whether a theory-only arm existed, for Derex et al. (2019) and Osiurak et al. (2021, 2022). Full text was unreachable.
- None of the wheel studies changes the physics partway through a chain (for example, a new track or a new optimum). So adaptation latency after environmental change has never been measured in this paradigm.

## Q2. Other human experiments comparing transmitted theories, explanations or goals with behaviors, procedures or demonstrations

### Takeaway
The strongest study on the environmental-change question is Roberts-Gaal, Bolic & Cushman (2025, *PNAS*). In an iterated-learning task, microcultures pass on procedures ("what to do") when the world is stable and pass on goals and causal information ("what to aim for and why") when it varies. This matches a tradeoff in which procedures are cheap but inflexible and reasons are flexible but costly. Other work shows that verbal teaching beats imitation or emulation for fidelity (Morgan et al., 2015), and that passing on *beliefs* rather than *behavior* lets knowledge accumulate (Beppu & Griffiths, 2009).

### Cited Findings
- **Roberts-Gaal X., Bolic M., Cushman F.A. (2025).** "Environmental variability shapes the representational format of cultural learning." *PNAS* 122(28), e2505283122, published 7 July 2025.
  - Contrasts learning "causally opaque procedures" with learning "goals and causal reasoning".
  - "Learning procedures is cheap but inflexible; learning goals is more flexible to changing circumstance, but requires expensive individual learning."
  - Online iterated-learning fishing task: 12 microcultures, 6 generations, about 1,093 participants (about 15 per microculture-generation). Participants leave written advice for the next generation.
  - In the variable condition, the bait mappings reverse every generation.
  - Result: advice was procedural when the world was stable and centred on outcomes and causes when it was variable.
  - Sources: [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2505283122); [PubMed 40623192](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40623192/)
- **Beppu A., Griffiths T.L. (2009).** "Iterated learning and the cultural ratchet." *Proceedings of the 31st Annual Conference of the Cognitive Science Society*.
  - Introduced "posterior passing": each learner passes its posterior on as the next learner's prior, which is equivalent to one learner seeing all the evidence.
  - Model and experiment suggest knowledge accumulates "if learners have access to the actual beliefs of their teachers, as opposed to merely observations of behavior informed by those beliefs."
  - Sources: [eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/24w0t8c9); [PhilPapers](https://philpapers.org/rec/BEPILA); description as given in [Prystawski et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18220)
- **Prystawski B., Arumugam D., Goodman N.D. (2025).** "Lossy communication constrains iterated learning." arXiv:2511.18220. Varying the rate of the belief-transmission channel produces "dramatic, non-linear changes to population performance". Includes a theoretical bound on how lossy communication constrains iterated learning. This is the closest formal treatment of an **information budget** for transmitting beliefs. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18220); [code](https://github.com/benpry/lossy-iterated-learning)
- **Morgan T.J.H., et al. (2015).** "Experimental evidence for the co-evolution of hominin tool-making teaching and language." *Nature Communications* 6:6029.
  - Oldowan knapping passed along chains of 5 or 10 people, N = 184, five transmission mechanisms.
  - "Transmission improves with teaching, and particularly with language, but not with imitation or emulation."
  - Sources: [Nat Commun](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms7029); [St Andrews portal](https://research-portal.st-andrews.ac.uk/en/publications/experimental-evidence-for-the-co-evolution-of-hominin-tool-making/)
- **Sumers T.R., Ho M.K., Hawkins R.D., Griffiths T.L. (2023).** "Show or tell? Exploring when (and why) teaching with language outperforms demonstration." *Cognition* 232: 105326. Language beats demonstration mainly when context is shared, because language can convey abstract knowledge that generalizes. — [PhilPapers](https://philpapers.org/rec/SUMSOT)
- **Thompson B., van Opheusden B., Sumers T., Griffiths T.L. (2022).** "Complex cognitive algorithms preserved by selective social learning in experimental populations." *Science* 376(6588): 95–98.
  - 3,450 participants, 20 populations, 12 generations, learning to sort an unknown sequence. Known sorting algorithms appeared.
  - Selective, success-biased social learning preserved efficient algorithms better than random social learning or asocial learning.
  - Sources: [PubMed 35357938](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35357938/); [Science](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abn0915)
- **Kalish M.L., Griffiths T.L., Lewandowsky S. (2007).** "Iterated learning: Intergenerational knowledge transmission reveals inductive biases." *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review*. Chains of function learners converge on their prior (positive linear functions) within 1–4 generations whatever the starting function. So transmitted structure drifts toward learner biases, not toward the truth. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/BF03194066)
- **Kirby S., Cornish H., Smith K. (2008).** "Cumulative cultural evolution in the laboratory: An experimental approach to the origins of structure in human language." *PNAS* 105(31): 10681–10686. Artificial languages passed down iterated-learning chains became easier to learn and more structured, with no intentional design. The transmitted rule system regularizes and compresses. — [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0707835105); [PubMed 18667697](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18667697/)
- **[UNVERIFIED, not searched in this session]** Other relevant experiments:
  - Caldwell & Millen (2009), *Psychological Science*, "Social learning mechanisms and cumulative cultural evolution: is imitation necessary?"
  - Zwirner & Thornton (2015), *Scientific Reports*, "Cognitive requirements of cumulative culture"
  - Wasielewski (2014), *Journal of Cognition and Culture*, teaching vs imitation
  - Caldwell et al. (2020), *WIREs Cognitive Science*, review of experimental methods ([Wiley](https://wires.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/wcs.1516), link seen in search)
  - Mesoudi & Whiten (2008), *Phil Trans B*, "The multiple roles of cultural transmission experiments" ([PubMed 18801720](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18801720/), link seen in search)

### Inferences
- Roberts-Gaal et al. (2025) directly supports the view that **reasons help adaptation under change**. It is observational about *what people choose to pass on*: format is not manipulated at a fixed budget, and false-belief persistence is not measured.
- Together, Derex et al. (2025) and Roberts-Gaal et al. (2025) set up the tension in the research question:
  - reasons canalize, so false beliefs persist in stable worlds;
  - reasons are preferred, and presumably useful, in variable worlds.
- No human study found here crosses these two factors (reason vs verdict × stable vs changing environment × accurate vs false seed theory).

### Gaps
- Whether Roberts-Gaal et al. measured how fast microcultures recovered after reversals, or how long false advice persisted, is not visible in the abstract.
- No human experiment was found that keeps the message length or bit budget equal between "theory" and "verdict" messages.

## Q3. Formal models: what is transmitted (rule/theory vs behavior) under environmental change

### Takeaway
The classical models (Rogers 1988; Boyd & Richerson 1985; Enquist, Eriksson & Ghirlanda 2007) transmit *behaviors* and treat environmental change as the reason pure copying goes stale. "Critical social learning", meaning copy and then check individually, resolves Rogers' paradox. The Bayesian iterated-learning line (Griffiths & Kalish; Beppu & Griffiths; Prystawski et al., 2025) is where "transmit beliefs/posteriors vs transmit behavior/data" is formalized. I found no model that compares reason transmission with verdict transmission at matched information and measures adaptation latency after change together with persistence of false beliefs.

### Cited Findings
- **Rogers A.R. (1988).** "Does biology constrain culture?" *American Anthropologist* 90(4): 819–831. Source of "Rogers' paradox": in a changing environment, social learning at equilibrium does not raise mean population fitness above that of individual learners. — [Wiley](https://anthrosource.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1525/aa.1988.90.4.02a00030); [Enquist et al. summary](https://www.jstor.org/stable/27563823)
- **Enquist M., Eriksson K., Ghirlanda S. (2007).** "Critical social learning: A solution to Rogers's paradox of nonadaptive culture." *American Anthropologist* 109(4): 727–734. A strategy that learns socially first and switches to individual learning if the copied behavior is unsatisfactory "always proves superior to pure social learning and typically has higher fitness than pure individual learning." — [JSTOR](https://www.jstor.org/stable/27563823); [Wiley](https://anthrosource.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1525/aa.2007.109.4.727)
- **Griffiths T.L., Kalish M.L. (2007).** "Language evolution by iterated learning with Bayesian agents" (*Cognitive Science*). Chains of posterior-sampling learners converge to the prior. — referenced in [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/280780736_Language_evolution_by_iterated_learning_with_Bayesian_agents) and [Kalish et al. 2007](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/BF03194066)
- **Beppu & Griffiths (2009)** and **Prystawski, Arumugam & Goodman (2025)**: see Q2. Passing posteriors or beliefs allows a ratchet; passing behavior or data does not; a lossy channel constrains performance non-linearly. — [eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/24w0t8c9); [arXiv 2511.18220](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18220)
- **Keßler S., Bautista-Salinero M., Tennie C., Wu C.M. (2026).** "Emergent social transmission of model-based representations without inference." arXiv:2604.05777, also on eScholarship (probably CogSci 2026).
  - RL simulations in a *reconfigurable* environment. A naive learner copies an expert's actions or boosts value representations, with no mental-state inference.
  - "Model-based learners benefit most from social exposure, showing faster learning and more expert-like representations."
  - Structured, reason-like representations can be transmitted indirectly through behavior.
  - Sources: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05777); [eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/0n99r1w4)
- **Yaman, Tian & Lindström (2026, PNAS).** Agent-based model in which semantic knowledge directs exploration and generalization. — [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2530750123)
- **[UNVERIFIED, not searched]** Further models:
  - Boyd R., Richerson P.J. (1985), *Culture and the Evolutionary Process* (University of Chicago Press): guided variation, biased transmission, and variable environments.
  - Boyd & Richerson (1995), "Why does culture increase human adaptability?", *Ethology and Sociobiology* 16: 125–143: shows social learning raises mean fitness only when it makes individual learning more accurate or cheaper.
  - Aoki, Wakano & Feldman (2005), *Theoretical Population Biology*, and Wakano, Aoki & Feldman (2004): learning schedules under temporally varying environments.
  - Kandler & Laland (2009), *Theoretical Population Biology*, and Kandler & Steele: innovation diffusion.
  - Kendal J., Giraldeau & Laland (2009), *Journal of Theoretical Biology*, "The evolution of social learning rules: payoff-biased and frequency-dependent biased transmission".
  - Rendell et al. (2010), *Science*, social learning strategies tournament: copying works well even with change.
- Stationary social learning in a changing environment (arXiv:2201.02122) appeared in search but was not examined. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.02122)

### Inferences
- In the classical theory, *what* is transmitted is always a behavior or trait. "Understanding" enters only as individual learning (guided variation, critical social learning). Transmitting a *rule* that generalizes to new environmental states is not a standard variable in that literature.
- The Bayesian iterated-learning literature supplies the formal machinery: posterior passing vs data passing, convergence to priors, lossy channels. But its canonical results assume a *fixed* true hypothesis. Environmental change combined with belief passing appears to be open ground.
- The convergence-to-prior result (Kalish et al., 2007) predicts that transmitted theories drift toward learners' biases. This mechanism is consistent with the "useless theories preserved" finding (Derex et al., 2025).

### Gaps
- I could not find, or verify, a model in which agents transmit an explicit causal rule vs an action, the environment changes, and both adaptation latency and false-belief persistence are measured at equal information cost. Nothing like this appeared in any search.

## Q4. Multi-agent RL, machine learning and LLMs: explanations vs actions

### Takeaway
In ML, explanations or language are shown to improve *learning* of relational and causal structure and generalization (Lampinen et al., 2022; Yu, Goodman & Mu, 2023). Cultural transmission in RL agents is shown through imitation of behavior (Bhoopchand et al., 2023) or generational accumulation (Cook et al., 2024). LLM transmission chains show that content drifts toward attractors (Perez et al., ICLR 2025). None of these compares reasons with verdicts under environmental change or measures how long false beliefs persist.

### Cited Findings
- **Lampinen A.K., Roy N., Dasgupta I., Chan S.C.Y., Tam A., McClelland J., Yan C., Santoro A., Rabinowitz N.C., Wang J., Hill F. (2022).** "Tell me why! Explanations support learning relational and causal structure." *ICML 2022*, PMLR 162: 11868–11890 (arXiv 2112.03753). Training RL agents to predict natural-language explanations as an auxiliary task lets them learn relational and causal structure they otherwise fail to learn. Explanations help the agent infer causal structure and help it generalize out of distribution. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/lampinen22a.html); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.03753v2)
- **Yu D., Goodman N.D., Mu J. (2023).** "Characterizing tradeoffs between teaching via language and demonstrations in multi-agent systems." arXiv:2305.11374, also on eScholarship (CogSci 2023).
  - Neural teacher and student agents; task difficulty and teacher competence are manipulated.
  - Demonstration works better on the easiest tasks. Language works better as difficulty rises "due to its ability to generalize more effectively to unseen scenarios".
  - Language is "more robust to teachers with low competence".
  - This is the closest ML analogue of reason vs conclusion transmission, but it is a single teacher-to-student step with no chain and no environmental change.
  - Sources: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11374); [eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97j4r1nn/qt97j4r1nn_noSplash_d4e427c1883bb381aa209b0201018bd9.pdf)
- **Bhoopchand A., Brownfield B., Collister A., Dal Lago A., Edwards A., Everett R., Fréchette A., Gitahy Oliveira Y., Hughes E., Mathewson K.W., Mendolicchio P., Pawar J., Pîslar M., Platonov A., Senter E., Singh S., Zacherl A., Zhang L.M. (2023).** "Learning few-shot imitation as cultural transmission." *Nature Communications* 14: 7536, published 28 November 2023. Agents imitate a human expert in real time in new 3D contexts without pre-collected human data. What is transmitted is **behavior**. — [Nat Commun](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-42875-2); [PubMed 38016945](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38016945/)
- **Cook J., Lu C., Hughes E., Leibo J.Z., Foerster J. (2024).** "Artificial Generational Intelligence: Cultural Accumulation in Reinforcement Learning." *NeurIPS 2024* (arXiv 2406.00392). Balancing social and independent learning produces cultural accumulation across generations. Accumulating agents beat single-lifetime agents given the same total experience. Two notions of generation are used: in-context and in-weights. — [NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/6df3a719d99bd2479c04114d357003d0-Abstract-Conference.html)
- **Perez J., et al. (2025).** "When LLMs Play the Telephone Game: Cultural Attractors as Conceptual Tools to Evaluate LLMs in Multi-turn Settings." *ICLR 2025* (arXiv 2407.04503). LLM transmission chains over 50 generations. Small biases are amplified toward attractors, and open-ended instructions give stronger attraction. — [ICLR proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/dbdea7859f1d2fc10f2c9e79b8f5ae54-Paper-Conference.pdf); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04503)
- The same paper cites Acerbi & Stubbersfield, reporting that LLM chains reproduce human-like content biases (gender stereotypes, negativity, threat, biology-related content). **Year and venue UNVERIFIED**; I believe it is *PNAS* 2023, "Large language models show human-like content biases in transmission chain experiments". — [Perez et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04503)
- Seen in search but not examined: "Collective Innovation in Groups of Large Language Models" (arXiv 2407.05377; Nisioti et al. 2024, UNVERIFIED authors), "Model Collapse as Cultural Evolution" (arXiv 2605.23054), and "Learning to solve complex tasks by growing knowledge culturally across generations" (arXiv 2107.13377; Tessler et al. 2021, UNVERIFIED authors). The last one has humans pass on written messages across generations in games. — [arXiv 2107.13377](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.13377); [arXiv 2407.05377](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.05377)

### Inferences
- ML evidence consistently favors language and explanation for *generalization*. This is the mechanism through which reasons might speed adaptation after change. But the tests are single-step teaching or training, not chains in non-stationary environments.
- LLM chain work shows a transmission-level failure mode: attractors and bias amplification. This is the LLM-level analogue of "trapped in false beliefs", but it has not been studied as a function of reason vs verdict content.

### Gaps
- I found no MARL or LLM study that transmits "rule + evidence" messages vs "verdict" messages across generations while switching environment payoffs and measuring re-adaptation time.

## Q5. Is there a direct, budget-controlled comparison of reason vs verdict transmission measuring adaptation latency and false-belief persistence?

### Takeaway
**No such study was found** in human experiments, formal models, or ML, through September 2026. The closest pieces each cover part of the design:

| Study | What it covers | What it lacks |
|---|---|---|
| Derex et al. 2019, 2025 | Reason vs conclusion; persistence of false or useless theories | Static environment; no matched information budget |
| Roberts-Gaal et al. 2025 | Environmental variability vs representational format | Format chosen by participants, not manipulated |

### Inferences
- **Already answered:**
  - In static environments, reasons are not needed for cumulative improvement.
  - At best, reasons give an early speed-up.
  - Reasons canalize exploration and preserve false theories.
  - People spontaneously shift toward passing on reasons when environments vary.
  - Language and explanations improve generalization in single-step teaching.
- **Not answered:**
  - A controlled causal test of whether reason transmission shortens adaptation latency after an environmental change.
  - Whether it lengthens persistence of false beliefs after the change.
  - Both questions at a fixed information budget.
  - How the tradeoff depends on the rate of change and on the accuracy of the seed theory.
- The literature predicts a plausible crossover:
  - reasons should win when a *correct* theory generalizes to the new environment state (Roberts-Gaal; Yu et al.);
  - reasons should lose when the theory is wrong or one-dimensional, because canalization suppresses the exploration needed to notice the change (Derex 2019, 2025).
  This prediction is untested.
